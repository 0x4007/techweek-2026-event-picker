NYC Tech Week 2026 Agenda

Last updated: 2026-05-10

This is the working agenda for NYC Tech Week 2026. It is built around the operational calendar route, not the full RSVP list. Most high-signal events are still pending host approval, so treat this as the planning agenda until approval emails or Partiful status updates come in.

Operating Goal

Meet NYC-local founders and engineering leaders who are likely buyers or champions for UbiquityOS Accolades.

Primary ICP:

  • CTOs, VPs of engineering, engineering managers, DevEx/platform leaders, AI infra leads, and technical founders.
  • Companies in devtools, infrastructure, AI, enterprise SaaS, open source, and crypto/web3.
  • Teams where engineering work happens across GitHub, PR reviews, issues/specs, Slack, and code agents, and where managers need better evidence of useful contribution.

Product framing:

UbiquityOS Accolades rewards the work behind the code by turning GitHub and Slack evidence into source-linked contribution credit.

The goal is not to pitch every person. The goal is to find teams with a real pain around invisible engineering work, code review quality, AI-assisted development accountability, or manager reporting.

Daily Strategy

  • Monday: Warm up aggressively. Prioritize founder/operator rooms and open-source/devtools conversations. Use backups if pending approvals do not clear.
  • Tuesday: Highest-priority day. The 16:00-21:00 block has the strongest ICP density: Copilot/control-plane, DevEx, Enterprise AI, Cloudflare/Shopify, Operating MCP.
  • Wednesday: Deep AI/devtools day. Keep the registered coding-agents fireside as the anchor. Swap only for a higher-signal curated approval.
  • Thursday: Keep energy for late afternoon/evening. The registered "Stop Making AI Guess" event is the anchor unless a substantially better curated dinner approves.
  • Friday: Taper. Bare Metal Happy Hour is worthwhile if approved; otherwise use Friday as a recovery and follow-up day.

Conversation Plan

Opening lines:

  • "I am working on UbiquityOS Accolades. It turns GitHub and Slack engineering artifacts into source-linked contribution credit, especially for work that commit counts miss."
  • "I am trying to understand how engineering managers are tracking useful work now that more code is being produced by agents and copilots."
  • "I am looking for teams where issue specs, PR reviews, Slack decisions, and code-agent workflows are becoming hard to attribute or audit."

Qualification questions:

  1. "How does your team currently recognize work that does not show up as commits?"
  2. "Do PR reviews, issue specs, or architecture comments matter in performance reviews or planning?"
  3. "Has AI coding changed what managers need to inspect before trusting output?"
  4. "Where do important engineering decisions live: GitHub, Slack, Linear/Jira, docs, or somewhere else?"
  5. "Do you have any internal system for contribution evidence, or is it mostly manager memory?"
  6. "Would source-linked credit be useful for rewards, reviews, audits, or just visibility?"
  7. "Who owns this problem on your team: engineering leadership, DevEx, platform, or people ops?"

High-signal buying triggers:

  • They mention difficulty assessing AI-generated or AI-assisted work.
  • They care about PR review quality, issue quality, or engineering planning quality.
  • They have remote/distributed teams and lose context in Slack/GitHub.
  • They already use GitHub heavily and have engineering managers who need reporting.
  • They dislike surveillance dashboards but need auditable contribution evidence.
  • They run open-source, bounty, contributor, or contractor workflows.

Follow-up ask:

  • "If this is relevant, can I send you a short write-up and ask for 20 minutes after Tech Week?"
  • "Who on your team would care most about source-linked engineering contribution evidence?"
  • "Would you be open to sanity-checking the product framing?"

Follow-Up Triage

Use this after every event.

Priority A:

  • CTO, VP engineering, engineering manager, DevEx/platform lead, or technical founder.
  • Clear pain around contribution attribution, AI code review, PR/spec quality, Slack decisions, or engineering reporting.
  • Next action: ask for a specific post-Tech Week call.

Priority B:

  • Founder/operator or senior engineer with relevant context but no obvious buying authority.
  • Next action: send the one-paragraph Accolades summary and ask for an intro to the engineering leader.

Priority C:

  • Interesting but weak ICP, investor, student, vendor, or general founder.
  • Next action: connect lightly; do not spend deep follow-up time unless they can introduce a buyer.

Log immediately:

  • Name
  • Company
  • Role
  • Event ID
  • Pain signal
  • Buying authority
  • Follow-up promised
  • Email/LinkedIn
  • Next action date
